Every person is different

The size of the gap, the teeth and the plan can be very different from person to person. So the facts here are general. They are not advice for one person. Before any treatment, the cleft team will talk with each person and family in detail about the benefits, risks and choices.

What was done

In alveolar bone grafting, new bone is put into the gap in the gum bone. This new bone is the bone graft. It is the person's own bone, taken from another part of the body.1 The gum is then closed over it.

This model shows the mouth some months after the operation. The bone graft is a lighter colour, so it is easy to see. In real life it slowly joins with the bone around it.

Checking the bone graft

Some months after the operation, an X-ray is often taken. It shows how much new bone has grown in the gap. One study took this X-ray at about 6 months.2

How well does it work?

A review of 8 studies checked the bone graft at least 1 year after the operation. It found:3

About 3 in 4bone grafts were a success on X-ray
About 8 in 10canines came through the new bone by themselves

Hospitals in Australia and the UK had high success rates, in line with other countries.4 Sometimes a second bone graft is needed. In one large study, this was about 1 in 100.5

What can make it less likely to work

A review of 9 studies found the bone graft was less likely to work when:6

  • it was done at an older age
  • the teeth were not kept clean
  • a tooth next to the gap had already come through

So the cleft team plans the time with care, and good tooth brushing helps.6

The canine tooth

Soon after the operation, the adult canine is still under the gum. This is normal. Now it has bone to come through. Most often it comes through by itself.3 Sometimes it needs help. A small operation can uncover the tooth, and braces can then pull it into place.7

Questions to ask the cleft team

  • When will the bone graft be checked?
  • How should the teeth be cleaned near the graft?
  • Is the canine on track to come through?
  • When might braces start?
