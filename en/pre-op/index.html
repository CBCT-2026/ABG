Every person is different

The size of the gap, the teeth and the plan can be very different from person to person. So the facts here are general. They are not advice for one person. Before any treatment, the cleft team will talk with each person and family in detail about the benefits, risks and choices.

What is a cleft lip and palate?

A cleft is a gap. Some babies are born with a gap in the top lip, the gum and the roof of the mouth (the palate). The gap forms before birth. It can be on one side or on both sides. This model shows a cleft on one side.

Operations as a baby

In the first year of life, operations close the lip and the roof of the mouth. The lip is most often closed at about 3 to 6 months of age. The palate is most often closed at about 6 to 12 months.12 In one children's hospital in Australia, the average age was about 5 months for the lip and about 18 months for the palate.1

These operations close the skin, gum and lining of the mouth. This is the soft tissue. They do not fill the gap in the bone of the gum. In this model, the soft tissue is a cover that can be taken off.

The gap in the gum bone

The upper gum has bone inside it. This bone holds the teeth. Its medical name is the alveolar bone. A gap in this bone is called an alveolar cleft.

Sometimes a small hole is left between the mouth and the nose. Food or drink can then go into the nose. This hole is called a fistula.3

Why alveolar bone grafting is done

Alveolar bone grafting is an operation. It puts new bone into the gap in the gum bone. This new bone is the bone graft. It is the person's own bone, taken from another part of the body.1 The bone graft:31

  • gives bone for the adult canine tooth to come through
  • holds up the teeth next to the gap
  • joins the two parts of the upper gum into one
  • closes a fistula, if there is one

When it is done

It is most often done at about 9 to 12 years of age.1 In the same Australian hospital, the average age was about 11.1 The time is chosen by how much the root of the adult canine has grown. The best time is when the root is about half to two thirds grown, before the tooth comes through.14

Before the operation, some children wear braces to make the upper jaw wider.5

The missing side tooth

The lateral incisor is the small tooth between the big front tooth and the canine. In about half of children with a cleft on one side, the lateral incisor next to the gap never forms.6 A tooth that never forms is called a missing tooth. The medical words are tooth agenesis or hypodontia. When this tooth does form, it is often small or an odd shape.6 By the end of treatment, the lateral incisor on the cleft side is often gone. One big hospital found this in more than 9 in 10 children.7

Missing adult teeth are common with a cleft. A review of 7 studies found that about 1 in 3 people with a cleft lip and palate had at least one.8

What happens next

After the bone graft, the adult canine most often comes through the new bone by itself. A review of many studies found this in about 8 in 10.9 In a study at a hospital in Melbourne, it came through in 26 of 28.10 If the lateral incisor is missing, the canine can move into its space.7 Later, most people have braces to line up the teeth.511

Questions to ask the cleft team

  • How big is the gap in the gum bone?
  • When is the best time for the bone graft?
  • Is the lateral incisor there, or is it missing?
  • Are braces needed before the operation?
  • Where will the new bone come from?
