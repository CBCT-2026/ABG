=== SHEET 1 === Stage 1: Before surgery Bone graft for a cleft in the gum: the three stages

[Picture: Whole jaw, with the gum. Labels: Gum (you can take it off); Front tooth (central incisor); Missing tooth (lateral incisor)]
[Picture: Gum taken off: the gap in the bone. Labels: Gap in the bone (alveolar cleft); Front tooth (central incisor); Missing tooth (lateral incisor)]
[Picture: From below: the roof of the mouth. Labels: Roof of the mouth (palate); Front tooth (central incisor)]
[Picture: Left side: the eye tooth window. Labels: Eye tooth window (canine)]
What this model shows
The gap is in the bone of the gums, next to the front teeth. Its medical name is the alveolar cleft.
In this model, one tooth on the left side never grew. It is the small tooth next to the front tooth (the lateral incisor). When a tooth never grows, the medical name is agenesis. About half of children with a cleft do not have this tooth on the cleft side.
At about 9 to 10 years old, you have both baby teeth and adult teeth. The adult front teeth and first back teeth are through. The baby eye teeth and baby back teeth are still in place. The adult eye teeth and the teeth behind them are still growing in the bone.
The pink part is the gum. It covers the gap. This is how it looks after surgery on the lip and the roof of the mouth in early life.
The pictures show the jaw with and without the gum.
The eye tooth
The adult eye tooth ( canine) is still high up in the bone next to the gap. It leans toward the gap. Its root is about half grown.
Over the next few years it moves down. It needs bone to grow into.
A small window in the bone on the left side lets you see it.
Why surgeons do the bone graft
The graft aims to: join the two parts of the upper jaw give the eye tooth bone to come down into hold up the teeth next to the gap and the side of the nose close any hole between the mouth and the nose
When surgeons do the graft
Most teams do the graft at about 8 to 11 years old. They pick the time by how far the teeth have grown, not by age.
The best time is before the eye tooth comes through the gum. Then its root is about 1/4 to 2/3 grown. Grafting then gives the most bone next to the teeth.
The operation
The surgeon takes soft, spongy bone, most often from the hip. They pack it into the gap and close the gum over it.

What has changed
The graft fills the gap from the floor of the nose down to the gum. It joins the two parts of the upper jaw.
The surgeon closed the gum over the graft with stitches. If the wound opens, you can lose some of the graft. Surgeons close the gum with care, because a wound that opens is the main problem after this operation.
The eye tooth is still in the bone. It is a little lower than before. Over the next months it moves down toward the graft.
Checks after surgery
You will have an X-ray at about 3 to 6 months. It shows how well the new bone has taken.
Some of the graft shrinks over time. Teeth that move into the graft help keep the bone.
The hip
Your hip can be sore for a while after the surgeon takes the bone. In one study, children went back to school about 2 weeks after surgery.

What has changed
Bone now joins the two parts of the jaw where the gap was.
The eye tooth came down through the graft. It is now fully in the row of teeth.
Most eye teeth come through on their own. Some get stuck. Then a small operation uncovers the tooth, and the braces slowly pull it down.
Fixed braces line up the teeth. Almost everyone who has this graft needs them.
The missing tooth
There are two main plans for the gap left by the tooth that never grew.
Close the space.
Braces move the eye tooth into the place of the missing tooth. Then the dentist reshapes it and adds tooth-coloured filling so it looks like the missing tooth. Teams use this plan most often for children with a cleft.
Keep the space.
While you are still growing, a plate you can take out holds a false tooth in the gap. When you stop growing, your dentist can fill the gap for good with: an implant: a small post in the bone with a false tooth (a crown) on top a bridge: a false tooth with one wing glued to the back of the tooth next to the gap
The pictures show each choice.
Your cleft team will talk with you about which plan suits you.
