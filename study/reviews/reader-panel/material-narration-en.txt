=== Stage 1: Before surgery ===
This is the upper jaw of a child, about 9 to 10 years old. The child was born with a split in the lip and gum on the left, called a cleft.
Under the pink gum, there is a gap in the bone that holds the teeth.  [on screen labels: Gum]
The small tooth next to the left front tooth never grew. About half of children with a cleft do not have this tooth.  [on screen labels: Missing tooth, Front tooth]
High in the bone, the adult eye tooth waits to come down. It needs bone to grow into.  [on screen labels: Eye tooth window]
At about 8 to 11 years old, a surgeon fills the gap with bone, most often from the hip. This is a bone graft.  [on screen labels: Gap in the bone]
=== Stage 2: 3 months after ===
This is the same jaw, about 3 months after the bone graft.
New bone now fills the gap, from the floor of the nose down to the gum. It joins the two parts of the upper jaw. The gum has healed over it.  [on screen labels: New bone]
The eye tooth is still in the bone. Over the next months, it moves down toward the new bone.  [on screen labels: Eye tooth]
An X-ray at about 3 to 6 months shows how well the new bone has taken. Some of it shrinks over time. Teeth that move into it help keep the bone.  [on screen labels: New bone]
Your hip can be sore for a while after surgery.
=== Stage 3: Years later ===
This is the same jaw a few years later.
Bone now joins the two parts of the jaw. The eye tooth came down through the new bone into the row of teeth. Some eye teeth get stuck and need a small operation.  [on screen labels: Eye tooth, New bone]
Fixed braces line up the teeth.  [on screen labels: Braces]
The tooth that never grew leaves a gap. Often, braces move the eye tooth into the gap, and the dentist reshapes it. Or you keep the gap, with a false tooth on a plate you can take out. When you stop growing, an implant or a bridge can fill it. Your cleft team will help you choose.  [on screen labels: Implant, Bridge, Plate]
