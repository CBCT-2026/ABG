2D or 3D? Which helps you learn about bone grafts for a cleft in the gum?

Study title: Seeing the stages: comparing 2D, augmented reality, video and 3D printed education materials to explain alveolar bone grafting in cleft lip and palate.

What is a bone graft for a cleft in the gum?

Some babies are born with a cleft lip and palate. Surgeons close the lip and the roof of the mouth in early life. But a gap often stays in the bone that holds the teeth.

At about 8 to 11 years old, a surgeon fills the gap with bone, most often from the hip. This gives the adult eye tooth bone to come down into. Treatment takes years. So this study shows it in three stages: before surgery, about 3 months after, and years later.

About this study

This project is part of a PhD at Nagoya University. It is run with Professor Toko Hayakawa and Professor Nagato Natsume of Aichi Gakuin University. A dentist runs the study. It asks how people rate 2D and 3D ways to show this treatment. You look at four ways to explain the three stages. Then you rate and rank them.

Icons only. You see the real ones for the first time during the study.

Who can take part?

You can take part if you are 18 or over and in any of these groups:

  • Dental students
  • Medical students
  • Dentists
  • Doctors
  • Oral and maxillofacial surgeons
  • Other health workers, such as nurses, speech therapists and dental hygienists
  • Members of the public (no health care training)

You do not need to know about cleft lip and palate to take part.

What does taking part involve?

  1. Scan the QR code. Read about the study and agree online.
  2. Look at the four ways to explain, one at a time, for a short set time each. Each one shows all three stages.
  3. Answer short questions about each one, then rank all four.
  4. The whole session takes about [X] minutes.

Good to know

  • Taking part is your choice. You can stop at any time before you send your answers.
  • We do not ask for your name. Your answers do not show who you are. We keep them safe.
  • It costs you nothing to take part.
  • This study does not give you advice about your own health or teeth.
